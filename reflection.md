# Reflection

Working on this project helped me see accounting concepts as logic rather than just
rules to memorize. The core idea that **Net Profit = Income − Expenses** turned out to be
a single line of code, but getting there meant I first had to think clearly about how to
*classify* a transaction — is it something that increases what the business owns
(income/capital) or something that reduces it (an expense)? That's essentially the
accounting equation in action, just expressed through an `if/else` type field instead of
a T-account.

Building the categorization (Sales, Rent, Marketing, Inventory, Capital) also made me
understand why a chart of accounts exists in real bookkeeping — grouping similar
transactions is what makes it possible to answer questions like "where is most of my
money going?" instead of just staring at a long list of numbers. Seeing that answer
appear instantly as a pie chart, rather than something I'd have to calculate by hand,
showed me why real businesses use software instead of manual ledgers.

Finally, adding the chatbot layer taught me that an "AI agent" doesn't have to be
complicated — even simple keyword matching can turn a static spreadsheet into something
that responds to questions in something close to plain language, which is the direction
real accounting software is heading.
